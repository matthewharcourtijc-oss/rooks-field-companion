# HARCOM Knowledge Content — Editorial Standard

The offline library is a safety-oriented reference, not a substitute for emergency services, professional training or local authority instructions.

## Required article fields

Every article must include:
- Stable unique `id`
- Plain-English `title` and short `summary`
- `body` that explains the situation without burying the action
- `categoryId`, searchable `tags` and one or more `sourceIds`
- `steps`: clear, ordered actions
- `equipment`: required or useful equipment; use an empty list when none is needed
- `commonMistakes` and `dangers`
- `stopAndGetHelp`: clear stop conditions and escalation route
- `contentBasis`: `official-guidance`, `established-practice` or `field-note`
- `jurisdiction`: UK-wide only where justified; otherwise name the nation or region
- `safetyLevel`: `info`, `caution` or `high`
- `reviewedAt`: ISO date
- `sourceIds`: one or more exact references

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:
1. UK legislation, government departments, regulators and emergency services.
2. NHS, Resuscitation Council UK, St John Ambulance and other recognised clinical authorities.
3. Met Office, Environment Agency, SEPA, Natural Resources Wales, DAERA/NIEA and other official national bodies.
4. Ordnance Survey, Mountain Rescue, RNLI, Food Standards Agency and recognised technical organisations.
5. Established practical references only where official guidance does not cover the technique.

Record the publisher, exact page title, canonical HTTPS URL and access date. Use multiple independent sources for high-risk advice where practical. Do not treat a source link as proof that every statement in an article is supported; editors must check the actual claim.

## Safety and wording rules

- Write original concise summaries; do not copy large sections of source text.
- Separate official guidance from established practical technique and personal field notes.
- State the jurisdiction. Do not present England-specific law or guidance as UK-wide.
- Medical content must be conservative, sourced and reviewed. Never improvise diagnosis, medication doses or advanced treatment instructions.
- Never tell a reader that a wild plant, berry or fungus is safe to eat based only on a picture or casual description.
- Knots and cordage articles must state that ordinary camp knots are not climbing, abseiling, rescue or life-safety systems unless supported by qualified specialist sources.
- Water articles must distinguish microbial contamination from chemical contamination. Boiling does not make chemically contaminated water safe.
- Give a clear "stop and get help" route. Never encourage a reader to take unnecessary risks to save property or equipment.
- Avoid absolute guarantees such as "safe", "foolproof", "always works" or "survival guaranteed".

## Review policy

- Record the date the article was checked against its sources.
- High-risk medical, water, weather, fire and rescue guidance should be reviewed at least every 6 months, and immediately when a cited authority changes its advice.
- Other articles should be reviewed at least annually.
- If a source becomes unavailable or guidance conflicts, mark the article for review; do not silently guess.
- Every content release must be validated as a whole before it can be signed and distributed.
